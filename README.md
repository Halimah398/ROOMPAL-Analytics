An in-depth descriptive data analysis of housing and roommate experiences among Nigerians, focusing on shared living, rental challenges, housing search stress, and trust in digital housing platforms.

📋 Project Overview
This project presents a comprehensive analysis of primary survey data collected from the “Apartment, House-Sharing & Roommate Experience Survey” in Nigeria. Using Microsoft Excel, the analysis transforms raw responses into actionable insights regarding:

Demographic profiles of respondents
Current living arrangements (alone, family, shared housing)
Housing search behaviors and stress levels
Shared housing experiences and roommate dynamics
Trust, fraud concerns, and adoption of digital housing platforms

🎯 Objectives

Understand who participates in shared/rental housing in Nigeria
Identify key pain points in the housing search process
Analyze trust barriers and verification needs in digital housing platforms
Provide data-driven recommendations for PropTech platforms and stakeholders

🛠️ Methodology

Tool: Microsoft Excel (Pivot Tables, Pivot Charts, Data Cleaning)
Dataset: Primary survey responses (Clean Data sheet + Data Dictionary)
Techniques:
Data cleaning & standardization
Descriptive statistics via Pivot Tables
Pivot Charts for visualization
Cross-tabulation by demographics (age, gender, employment, location)

No advanced statistical modeling was used the focus was on clear, transparent, and reproducible descriptive analysis.

📊 Key Findings
Demographics & Living Situation

Respondents are predominantly young/working-age and urban.
Employment status and age strongly influence living arrangements.
Shared housing is very common, especially among students and early-career individuals.

Housing Search Experience

Housing search is highly stressful for most respondents.
Stress persists even among experienced renters → indicates systemic market issues.
Digital platforms are the most used but face significant trust challenges.

Trust & Security

Fraud is a major concern.
Verified listings, identity verification, and secure communication are the top trust-building features.
Lack of trust is the primary reason many avoid housing platforms.

Shared Housing

Widely used but sensitive major concerns include compatibility, safety, cleanliness, and accountability.
Most roommate connections happen through informal networks.

💡 Recommendations

Prioritize Trust Infrastructure — Verified listings, identity checks, and transparent profiles are non-negotiable.
Demographic Segmentation — Tailor solutions for students, young professionals, etc.
Improve Shared Housing Matching — Add compatibility tools and clear expectation-setting features.
Increase Transparency — Standardized listing information to reduce search stress.
Bridge Informal & Digital Channels — Build platforms people actually trust enough to replace word-of-mouth.

Real Estate Professionals: Better understanding of renter behavior
Housing Advocates & Policymakers: Evidence of systemic housing search challenges
